The BMW M140i is one of the best-value performance cars BMW has ever built - a 3.0-litre turbocharged straight-six squeezed into a compact hatchback, driving the rear wheels (or all four on xDrive cars). The B58 engine under the bonnet is a genuine tuner's favourite, and it's exactly the kind of car we love to see roll into the workshop.

This one arrived by a slightly unusual route, though. It didn't come in for a remap in the first instance - it came in because of a faulty BCM.

## The B58: why the M140i responds so well to a remap

BMW ships the M140i's B58 in a deliberately conservative state of tune. From the factory it makes around 340bhp, but the engine, turbo and fuelling have far more to give - which is why a Stage 1 remap is one of the most popular upgrades on these cars.

Stage 1 is a software-only change that stays within the limits of the standard hardware: no new parts, and it doesn't materially compromise reliability when it's done properly - just the headroom BMW left on the table, unlocked.

## Does a BCM repair wipe your remap?

Not directly - but it's how this M140i ended up needing a fresh one. The customer's first problem wasn't the tune at all; it was a faulty **BCM (body control module)**. The BCM looks after a huge range of a car's electrical functions, and when one starts to fail it can throw up a frustrating spread of gremlins.

It's a specialist repair, so rather than send the customer round the houses we put them in touch with a company we work with regularly and trust for exactly this kind of module work. The repair sorted the electrical fault properly.

The knock-on effect, though, was that the car ended up back on its standard factory map. The Stage 1 the M140i had been running before was gone. That's not unusual when module and software work is carried out - the safe path often means returning the ECU to standard, and any existing tune goes with it.

## A clean slate: fitting a fresh Stage 1

With the BCM repaired and the car back on factory software, the customer wanted its performance back. So they came to us for a fresh Stage 1.

The process was the same one every remap gets:

- A **diagnostic health check first** - a paid check, not a free once-over - to confirm the engine was healthy and fault-free before we touched the ECU.
- A **full backup of the original file**, so the car can be returned to standard at any time.
- A **Stage 1 map matched to this exact car**, then post-remap checks and a road test to confirm it was all running cleanly.

## The results

On this M140i, the Stage 1 figures were:

- **Power: 340 → 400bhp** (around +60bhp)
- **Torque: 500 → 580Nm** (around +80Nm)

Numbers on a page are one thing, but it's how the car drives afterwards that owners notice most. That extra torque lands right where you use it - the mid-range - so overtakes need less planning, the throttle feels sharper, and the B58's already-smooth straight-six delivery becomes even more effortless.

Gains like these are typical of a healthy B58 at Stage 1, but every car is different. The exact figures depend on the vehicle's condition, fuel and spec, which is why we quote per car rather than promise a fixed number.

## Is a Stage 1 remap right for your M140i?

For most M140i owners, Stage 1 is the sweet spot. It needs no supporting hardware, keeps the car civilised for everyday use, and delivers the majority of the usable gain. A few things worth knowing before you book:

- **Insurance:** a remap is a modification and must be declared to your insurer. Plenty of specialist and mainstream insurers cover remapped cars at a sensible premium - just don't leave it undeclared.
- **Gearbox:** the ZF eight-speed auto takes Stage 1 torque in its stride; the six-speed manual is generally fine at Stage 1 too, though a tired clutch is the first thing to watch as torque climbs.
