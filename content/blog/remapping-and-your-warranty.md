Does remapping void your warranty? It can. A remap rarely cancels the whole warranty in one go, but warranty terms commonly exclude unapproved engine software and the damage it causes - so engine, turbo, clutch and gearbox claims are the ones most likely to be refused. Some used-car warranties will not cover a modified car at all.

Which cover you have matters. A manufacturer warranty, a used-car or extended warranty and your rights against the business that supplied the car are three separate things, and a remap affects each one differently - so start by checking which one you are relying on.

## Does remapping void your warranty on a new car?

Manufacturer warranty terms commonly exclude parts, equipment or software that were not fitted when the car was built, and damage caused by alteration or modification. Volkswagen's published UK new-car warranty terms, for example, list unapproved software changes such as chip tuning among the items not covered. Kia's UK terms place alteration and modification among the causes of damage they exclude.

Read closely, many of these clauses exclude the modification and the damage linked to it rather than cancelling every part of the warranty. That distinction matters less than it sounds.

An [ECU remap](/ecu-remapping) changes how the engine produces torque, so the claims most likely to be disputed are exactly the expensive ones: engine internals, turbocharger, injection system, clutch, dual-mass flywheel and gearbox. A failed window motor is unlikely to be argued over. A failed turbo on a mapped car very likely will be.

The terms, not opinions on a forum, decide where the line sits for your car. If your warranty booklet is unclear, ask the manufacturer or dealer in writing and keep the reply.

## What about a used car warranty?

Used-car and extended warranties are separate contracts - sometimes supplied by the dealer with the car, sometimes bought direct, and some are insurance policies in their own right. Their terms vary widely, and some are stricter than manufacturer cover.

Some products exclude any vehicle modified from the manufacturer's original specification, which can mean a remapped car is simply not eligible - not just that some claims are refused. Volkswagen's own Approved Used warranty booklet, for example, excludes vehicles modified in any way from the original manufacturer's specification. Others exclude damage caused by modifications in a similar way to a manufacturer warranty, and some specialist providers offer cover aimed at modified cars.

Before booking a remap on a car with a used-car warranty, check:

1. **Eligibility.** Does the policy exclude modified vehicles outright?
2. **Exclusions.** Is damage caused by a modification, or by engine software changes, excluded?
3. **Notification.** Are you required to tell the provider about changes to the vehicle?
4. **Servicing conditions.** Many warranties also require servicing to the manufacturer's schedule. It is a separate condition, but one to keep in order either way.

If any answer is unclear, ask the provider in writing before the work, not after.

## Can dealers detect a remap?

Often, yes - and putting the original software back does not necessarily remove every trace.

Many ECUs keep programming records, typically a date and a code for the workshop or tool that carried out the programming. The diagnostic standard many modern ECUs use, ISO 14229 (Unified Diagnostic Services), defines standard identifiers for those records, though how each manufacturer uses them varies.

Dealer systems can also compare the part numbers and software versions recorded in each control unit with the manufacturer's records, and some manufacturers run separate checks for tuning. Technician bulletins published by Audi of America and Volkswagen Group of America describe this as Software Version Management: the dealer's diagnostic tool sends control unit data to a central system holding the last known good configuration for each VIN.

The same bulletins tell US dealers who find a tuned engine or gearbox control unit to explain to the owner that damage caused by the tuning will not be covered by the warranty. They are also told to get written consent before reprogramming, because the update may overwrite the tune.

> **Common misunderstanding:** "I will flash it back to standard before a warranty claim." Putting the [original ECU file](/blog/why-we-back-up-the-original-ecu-file) back restores the software, not the history - programming records may remain, and so does the period the car was mapped.

## Should you remap a car under warranty?

That is your decision, but it should be an informed one.

- **Inside a manufacturer warranty you rely on?** Think carefully. If an engine or gearbox claim would matter to you financially, the simplest option is often to wait until the warranty ends.
- **Want more power and full cover?** Some manufacturers have sold their own dealer-fitted performance software with warranty cover, so ask your dealer what is currently available for your model before anyone changes the map.
- **On a used-car warranty?** Read the terms before mapping. If the policy excludes modified vehicles, mapping may effectively end your cover.
- **Already mapped?** Do not hide it. Keep the paperwork and the original file, and be straightforward if you make a claim.
- **Claim refused because of a remap?** Ask for the refusal, and the clause it relies on, in writing. If that does not settle it, check whether the manufacturer or warranty provider is accredited to The Motor Ombudsman, which offers consumers a free dispute service under its codes for new cars and warranty products.
- **Insurance is a separate question.** A remap is a modification and should be declared to your insurer - [remapping and your insurance](/blog/remapping-and-your-insurance) covers what to tell them.

Condition matters as much as cover. A car with an existing fault is a poor candidate whether or not it is under warranty, which is why [vehicle condition is checked before any remap](/blog/why-vehicle-condition-matters-before-a-remap).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a tuning box safer for my warranty than a remap?

Not under typical terms. Volkswagen's UK new-car terms exclude components, equipment or software that were not part of the vehicle at the point of original manufacture, which on a plain reading covers a plug-in box as much as a remap. Removing the box before a dealer visit does not change what happened while it was fitted.

### Will a dealer software update remove my remap?

It can. A software update to the engine or gearbox control unit during a dealer visit may overwrite a remap, so ask what updates are planned before the car goes in. The US bulletins above tell dealers to get written consent when they find a tune, but ask rather than assume every workshop works that way.

### Does a remap affect my rights against the dealer that sold me the car?

The Consumer Rights Act 2015 gives you rights against the business that supplied the car - usually the dealer, or the finance company if you bought on hire purchase or PCP - not the manufacturer. Those rights relate to the car's condition when it was supplied, and Citizens Advice notes they are usually easiest to use in the first six months and can last up to six years (five in Scotland). A later remap does not change that condition, but it can make it harder to show that an engine or driveline fault was there from the start.

### Can I get warranty cover for a remapped car?

Some warranty providers offer products aimed at modified vehicles. Read the eligibility rules and exclusions as carefully as you would with any other policy, and declare the remap when you apply.

## The takeaway

A remap rarely cancels a warranty in one stroke, but it gives the warrantor grounds to dispute the engine and driveline claims that cost the most. Read your terms before mapping, keep the original file, and if a manufacturer warranty matters to you, consider waiting until it ends.
