## Why are some DPF cleaning quotes so much cheaper?

Three very different things get sold as "DPF cleaning", and they are not priced - or effective - in the same way.

- **An additive** is a product poured into the tank or sprayed into the filter. At best it helps soot burn off a lightly loaded filter on a healthy engine.
- **A forced regeneration** is a garage procedure using a diagnostic tool to burn soot off in place. It can help when the filter is soot-loaded and the vehicle allows it, but it leaves ash where it is.
- **An off-vehicle clean** physically removes soot and ash from the removed filter, and measures the result.

Soot burns; ash does not. So can you clean a DPF without removing it? Only in the sense of burning soot off - the ash stays in the filter until it is cleaned off the vehicle or the filter is renewed. We explain the difference fully in [when a DPF needs off-vehicle cleaning](/blog/when-does-a-dpf-need-off-vehicle-cleaning).

If a quote looks far cheaper than an off-vehicle clean, ask whether it is really an additive or a forced regeneration.

### Flow and back-pressure testing, before and after

A filter should be tested as it arrives and again once it is clean, on the same equipment. That comparison is the evidence the clean worked. A photo of a bright inlet face is not, because the deposits sit deep inside the channels. We cover each stage in [what happens during an off-vehicle DPF clean](/blog/what-happens-during-off-vehicle-dpf-cleaning).

### An honest assessment and report

Not every filter can be saved: a cracked, melted or collapsed substrate may not be recoverable. A good service inspects the filter, tells you plainly what it found and reports the test results - including when cleaning is not the right repair.

## What is usually priced separately?

These sit outside a filter-cleaning price, so ask about each one.

1. **Removal and refit labour.** This varies more than anything else. On some vehicles the filter is under the floor and comes off with a handful of fixings; on others it sits close to the turbocharger and other parts have to come off first.
2. **Finding out why it blocked.** A diagnostic check of fault codes and live data is separate work from cleaning the filter.
3. **Gaskets, clamps and fixings.** Gaskets, and on many vehicles some clamps and fixings, should be renewed as the workshop manual specifies.
4. **Resets after refitting.** On many vehicles the ECU's stored soot and ash values need resetting or relearning once a cleaned filter goes back on - see [resets and checks after a DPF clean](/blog/after-a-dpf-clean-resets-and-checks).
5. **Any faulty sensor or pipe.** A failed differential pressure sensor, a split pressure pipe or a faulty temperature sensor is a repair in its own right.
6. **Getting the filter to the cleaner.** Within 10 miles of Totnes, our collection and return is part of the £210. For a postal clean you book the courier to us yourself, so allow for that when you compare quotes.

## Is it cheaper to clean or replace a DPF?

Cleaning is usually far cheaper than a new filter, for a simple reason: you keep the part you already have. Most DPFs are a ceramic filter inside a steel canister, and many carry a catalyst coating containing precious metals such as platinum. On some vehicles the filter also shares a housing with the oxidation catalyst, so a new unit replaces both. Removal and refit labour is broadly the same either way, so the saving comes from the part.

Replacement is genuinely the answer when the substrate is cracked, melted or collapsed, and heavy oil or coolant contamination can make a filter unsuitable for reuse. That is exactly what pre-clean inspection and flow testing are for: they tell you whether you are paying to clean a serviceable filter or should be buying a new one.

## Why the cause matters to the real cost

A filter that blocks again costs you twice. Cleaning removes what is in the filter; it does nothing about whatever filled it. If an EGR fault, worn injectors, a sensor problem or a journey pattern that never lets a regeneration finish is still there, the clean filter soon loads up and blocks again.

That is why the real cost of DPF cleaning includes finding the cause. We explain the common reasons in [why a DPF blocks again after regeneration](/blog/why-dpf-blocks-again-after-regeneration). In Devon, our paid DPF diagnostics check reads the codes and live data, usually takes around an hour, and normally means bringing the vehicle to Totnes.

## Questions to ask before you pay anyone

1. Is the filter cleaned off the vehicle, and by what method?
2. Will it be flow tested before and after, and will I see the results?
3. What happens if the filter turns out to be cracked or melted?
4. Does the price include removal, refit, gaskets and the reset, or are those extra?
5. Has anyone established why it blocked, and will that be fixed?
6. Is the price confirmed for my exact vehicle?

If anyone suggests removing the filter instead of cleaning it, walk away. On a vehicle built with a DPF, [removal is not a legal repair](/blog/dpf-removal-and-the-law), and the vehicle will fail its MOT if the tester finds the filter has been removed.
