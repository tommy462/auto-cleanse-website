A motorhome remap or a remap for towing can make a heavy vehicle noticeably easier to drive: more torque low and mid-range, fewer downshifts on long hills and steadier cruising with a caravan or a full load behind you. What it cannot do is change what you are allowed to carry or tow. Plated weights, towing limits and your driving licence entitlement stay exactly where they were.

So is it worth it? It can be, on a healthy vehicle whose cooling, clutch and gearbox have margin to spare - after a health check, a look at your warranty terms and a word with your insurer.

> More torque makes a heavy load easier to pull. It does not make a heavier load legal.

## Is a motorhome remap worth it?

The driveability case for remapping a loaded or towing vehicle - fuller torque low down, fewer downshifts on gradients - is covered in [remapping for towing, payload and gearing](/blog/van-and-commercial-remapping). A motorhome or tow car is used differently from a working van, though: long, heavy trips with weeks or months of standing in between.

Devon is a fair test. Long, steep climbs on main roads and lanes alike are exactly where a heavy vehicle on standard calibration can feel laboured.

Three things decide whether it makes sense for yours:

- **Condition.** A health check comes first. Months in storage and stored faults both change the answer, as explained below.
- **The driveline.** The cooling system has to cope with sustained load, and the clutch and gearbox have to take the extra torque.
- **The paperwork.** Check your base vehicle warranty terms before the work. A remap is a modification and must be declared to your insurer - see [remapping and your insurance](/blog/remapping-and-your-insurance).

## Does a remap increase towing capacity?

No. A vehicle's maximum authorised mass (MAM, also called gross vehicle weight) is the most it may weigh with its load, and its gross train weight is the combined limit for vehicle, trailer and load. GOV.UK explains that the MAM is listed in the owner's manual and is normally shown on a plate or sticker on the vehicle, which may also show the gross train weight. The towing limit is usually given in the handbook.

Those figures are set when the vehicle is designed and type-approved, and changing the engine software does not change them. The only legitimate way a plated weight changes is formal re-plating - GOV.UK notes that a vehicle unlikely to be used at its potential maximum weight may be down-plated - never a remap.

On a tow car, a remap does not change the caravan-to-car weight match either. The Caravan and Motorhome Club recommends a towed load of no more than 85% of the car's kerbweight for beginners, perhaps up to 90% for experienced towers, and never more than 100%. That is a stability recommendation rather than law, and extra torque does nothing to the kerbweight it is based on.

On a motorhome, the gap between the weight as it is actually driven and the plated maximum can be small once water, gas, bikes and passengers are aboard. If you are not sure where yours sits, a weighbridge answers the question better than a feel for how the engine copes.

## What has to be respected

### Exhaust temperatures on long climbs

Sustained full load drives exhaust gas temperature up. Exhaust temperature is monitored to protect the turbocharger, catalyst and DPF from overheating, and engine management may reduce power to protect components if temperatures climb too far. A responsible calibration keeps those protections working. On a motorhome or tow car, it is the long climb, not the short burst, that the file has to be built around.

### Cooling, clutch and gearbox

A long climb with a caravan or a full motorhome keeps the engine working hard for minutes at a time. Volvo's owner guidance, for example, warns that the engine and cooling system can overheat on hilly terrain, in high temperatures or with a heavy load or trailer.

The clutch and gearbox then have to transmit whatever extra torque the engine makes, within limits of their own - [engine torque and gearbox limits](/blog/engine-torque-gearbox-limits-and-stage-1-calibration) explains why the driveline is part of the decision. If any of the three is marginal on standard calibration, it needs sorting before anyone asks it for more.

## Remapping a motorhome: base vehicles, gearboxes and storage

### Base vehicles

Many motorhomes are built on light commercial chassis. Fiat bills the Ducato as Europe's most popular motorhome base vehicle, and its Peugeot Boxer and Citroen Relay siblings share much of the same platform; the Ford Transit, Mercedes-Benz Sprinter and other large vans are common too. The engine and driveline are commercial units, but the way they are used is very different from a working van.

### Know which gearbox is fitted

Tell whoever quotes the remap which gearbox the vehicle has, because it changes the conversation. Ducato-based motorhomes built before the 2020 model year may have the Comfort-Matic, an automated (robotised) manual: a conventional clutch and gearbox operated by actuators, so the clutch point above applies to it as much as to a manual.

From the 2020 model year Fiat offered a torque-converter automatic instead: a nine-speed at first, and an eight-speed on the latest models. Each gearbox has its own torque limits and protection strategy, which a calibration must respect rather than override. If you are not sure which is fitted, check the vehicle's documents rather than going by the year alone.

### Months in storage

Many motorhomes sit for months, then cover long, heavy trips. That pattern brings problems a health check needs to catch:

- **Batteries.** Storage takes a toll on starter batteries, and a weak battery makes for unreliable starting.
- **The DPF.** Short trips to and from storage, or an engine started and idled now and then, give the DPF little chance to regenerate properly. A part-loaded filter should be investigated before a remap, not after.
- **Stored faults.** Sensor and battery-voltage faults can log while a vehicle stands. They need reading and resolving, not clearing.

## What licence do you need for a motorhome or towing?

A remap changes nothing here either. In Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales), GOV.UK and DVSA set out the basics:

- **Category B (car licence):** petrol and diesel vehicles up to 3,500kg MAM. If you passed your car test on or after 1 January 1997, since 16 December 2021 you can also tow a trailer up to 3,500kg MAM without a separate car and trailer (B+E) test.
- **Passed before 1 January 1997:** you can usually drive a vehicle and trailer combination up to 8,250kg MAM, and your licence will usually already include C1.
- **Motorhomes plated over 3,500kg:** these need category C1 up to 7,500kg MAM, or category C above that. From age 70, GOV.UK says C1 has to be renewed by post, not online.

Northern Ireland has its own rules - check nidirect. Whatever your licence allows, the vehicle's own limits still apply, so check the categories on your licence record rather than relying on memory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a remap void my motorhome's warranty?

It can affect it. Manufacturer warranty terms commonly exclude unapproved software changes and the damage linked to them, and engine, turbo, clutch and gearbox claims are the ones most likely to be disputed. Check your base vehicle warranty terms in writing first - [remapping and your warranty](/blog/remapping-and-your-warranty) explains what to look for.

### Will a remap improve fuel economy when towing?

It might, but nobody can honestly promise a figure in advance. On a heavy trip the load, the route and the way the vehicle is driven all move the result. [Realistic fuel savings from a remap](/blog/economy-remaps-and-realistic-fuel-savings) explains what a calibration can and cannot do for economy.

## The takeaway

A remap can make a motorhome or tow car calmer and more capable on long hills, but the plated weights and your licence stay exactly as they were. Get the health check done first, know which gearbox is fitted, check your warranty and tell your insurer, and treat the remap as a driveability improvement rather than extra capacity.
